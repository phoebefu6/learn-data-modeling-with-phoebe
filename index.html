SEC-02 The disagreement

The most useful 45 minutes in the track

You will sit in a room where two competent teams present two different numbers for the same word, and you will be expected to decide which one is right. Almost every time, neither team is wrong and neither is lying. They are counting different rows.

Every disagreement about a number is really a disagreement about grain.
The two questions that resolve it in the room
1 what does ONE ROW of this table mean? 2 per what? per order, per line, per merchant?

A fact table is where the numbers live, so it is the table people fight about. Almost every fight traces back to the same missing artifact: nobody wrote down what one row means.

  • plus a semantic
  • layer an agent

None of the four dimension rules is a style preference. Each exists because a specific, expensive failure happens without it.
How to read this against SEC-06

Each of the four dimension rules answers one of the four failures in the next module. The rules are not a house style; they are the shape those failures left behind.

SEC-06 Four expensive failures

History that rewrites itselfYesterday's report changes because an attribute was updated in place. Ask: can we still reproduce last quarter's number exactly?
A join count that grows with every questionEach new question needs one more hop, so nobody can answer anything quickly. Ask: how many joins does the common question take?
Today's rate against April's salesA dashboard applies the current commission rate to historical revenue. Ask: which version of this attribute was true then?
Quietly 4 percent shortA quarter of revenue reporting is wrong because one attribute was NULL. Ask: what happens to a row when this field is missing?
Where these are dealt with

Session b6 builds the six dimensions that prevent the first three. Session b7 writes the grain sentence that prevents the fourth from hiding. Session b10 takes the whole thing to returns and refunds, which is where grain stops being obvious.

These are the four reasons the dimension rules exist. They are not preferences, they are scar tissue.

SEC-07 The four questions

Why they are the exam

A model is not good because it is normalized, or star-shaped, or documented. It is good because the questions the business actually asks are cheap and unambiguous to answer with it.

1
ASK ITthe question as the business words it
2
ANSWER ITlive, against the star, in one query
3
WATCH ONE DIEa plausible hypothesis fails on the evidence
4
WATCH ONE SURVIVEand know why the model let you tell them apart
A rate without its denominator is not evidence. A model's real job is to make a hypothesis refutable.
